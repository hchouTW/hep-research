# References: transition radiation detectors

A split file of the annotated [bibliography](detector-principles-bibliography.md), in the same citation format: the sources of `transition-radiation-detectors.md`. Entries that name an experiment or apparatus sit inside example blocks.

## Transition radiation detectors

- Particle Data Group (S. Navas et al.), "Passage of particles through matter" and "Particle detectors at accelerators", in Review of Particle Physics, Phys. Rev. D 110 (2024) 030001, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.110.030001, and the 2025 updates, <https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-passage-particles-matter.pdf> and <https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-particle-detectors-accel.pdf> (read 2026-11-21). Sec. 34.7.3, eqs. 34.45-34.47, pp. 35-36: single-interface energy, plasma energy, spectral hardening, formation length, stack intensity proportional to N. Sec. 35.6.5, pp. 45-48 (Fig. 35.18 on p. 48): TR emission angle, threshold, saturation and peak formulas in analytic and practical numerical forms (p. 45), Z^n/omega^3 absorption, thick and thin designs, TR build-up, estimators, rejection versus detector length (p. 47), hadron separation, nuclei, silicon detection ([transition radiation detectors](transition-radiation-detectors.md)).
- A. Andronic and J. P. Wessels, "Transition radiation detectors", Nucl. Instrum. Methods A 666 (2012) 130, DOI 10.1016/j.nima.2011.09.041, arXiv:1111.4188 (read 2026-11-21 and 2026-11-22). Hadron identification in a 250 GeV beam (sec. 1, p. 2); formation zone, stack spectrum, spectrum peak and saturation (sec. 2.2, eqs. 5-9); irregular radiators (sec. 2.3); yield with absorption and hadron ranges (sec. 2.4, eq. 10); Xe absorption length versus energy (sec. 3.1, Fig. 4); likelihood and pion efficiency versus electron efficiency, layers and momentum (sec. 3.2, eqs. 12-13); radiator and detector design, with the TRD table (sec. 3.3, Table 1, p. 14); heavy nuclei and silicon TRD (sec. 3.4); delta-electron escape (sec. 4.3.1); fixed-target TRDs (sec. 4.4); balloon and space constraints (sec. 4.5).
- S. T. Alnussirat and M. L. Cherry, "Extending the Lorentz factor range and sensitivity of transition radiation with compound radiators", Nucl. Instrum. Methods A 1027 (2022) 166362, DOI 10.1016/j.nima.2022.166362, arXiv:2201.07901 (read 2026-11-21). Read in the abstract only: compound radiators of varying foil thickness and spacing to extend the Lorentz-factor range for TeV hadrons and cosmic-ray nuclei.
- J. Alozy et al., "Identification of particles with Lorentz factor up to 10^4 with Transition Radiation Detectors based on micro-strip silicon detectors", Nucl. Instrum. Methods A 927 (2019) 1, DOI 10.1016/j.nima.2019.02.032, arXiv:1901.11265 (read 2026-11-21 and 2026-11-22). Sec. 1: saturation about ten times a threshold near 5e2, attributed to the spacing-to-foil ratio (p. 2); separations of about a hundred um at about 1 m (p. 4), or silicon strips with a magnetic field at modest distances (p. 3); hadron separation needs longer TRDs and, at TeV, several thresholds or TR energy and angle; silicon strip measurement at a test beam, 2.2 m behind the radiator (sec. 2.2, p. 7).
