# Reviewer-style assessment

Use for a mock referee report, submission-readiness review, or requested critical
assessment. For responding to actual reviews, use `submission-and-peer-review.md`.
Identify the manuscript version and review scope. Apply a venue's rubric when
provided; retrieve current official criteria if venue-specific scoring is needed.

Begin with a neutral account of the question, contribution, and evidence, then
identify substantive strengths. Assess central validity, novelty relative to
verified prior work, comparison fairness, uncertainty, reproducibility, scope of
conclusions, and clarity. Use `claim-evidence-mapping.md` for disputed conclusions
and `reading-papers.md` for domain-specific checks rather than duplicating audits.

Classify concerns by consequence:

- Major: threatens a central inference, leaves a necessary comparison unresolved,
  or prevents assessing the claimed contribution.
- Minor: local ambiguity, presentation problem, or correction that does not change
  the central inference.

For each concern give a precise locator, evidence or reasoning, why it matters,
and the smallest action that would resolve it. Distinguish a demonstrated defect
from a question for the authors. Separate necessary validation from interesting
future work; avoid demanding an unrelated new research program.

Novelty judgments require comparing relevant primary sources. If that literature
has not been checked, qualify the judgment rather than declaring the result
unoriginal. Do not invent reviewer quotations, venue requirements, or acceptance
probabilities. Provide a recommendation or score only when requested or required
by the supplied rubric, together with confidence and limits of the review.

Deliver a concise contribution summary, strengths, prioritized concerns, and
actionable author questions. State whether supplements, code, and external
literature were inspected; do not imply they were reviewed when unavailable.
