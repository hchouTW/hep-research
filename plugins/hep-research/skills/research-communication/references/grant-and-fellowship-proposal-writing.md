# Grant and fellowship proposal writing

Use when drafting a grant or fellowship proposal (e.g. NSF/NIH-style
Specific Aims, a fellowship research statement, a telescope/beamtime
allocation proposal). This is a distinct document genre from a paper — it
argues for *future* work rather than reporting completed results — so treat
this as adjacent support, not an extension of the paper-writing workflow.
Reuse `scientific-style.md` for sentence-level prose guidance and the
argument-restructuring section of `paper-structure.md` for organizing an
argument around evidence; do not reuse a paper's section structure directly.

1. Get the call's actual requirements before drafting: page/word limit,
   required sections (Specific Aims, Significance, Innovation, Approach,
   Broader Impacts, budget justification, biosketch format), and the
   funder's specific review criteria — proposal review criteria are funder-
   specific and often published; retrieve the current version rather than
   assuming a prior cycle's format still applies.
2. Lead with the problem and the gap, then the proposed approach — a
   proposal's opening (Specific Aims page or equivalent) must be readable
   standalone, since some reviewers on a panel only read that page closely.
   State what will be done, not only what is wrong with the current state
   of the field.
3. Distinguish preliminary results (already-completed work supporting
   feasibility, presented briefly) from the proposed work (what the funding
   would newly enable) — do not let preliminary results crowd out space for
   the actual proposal, and do not present preliminary work as if it were
   already the funded outcome.
4. Write Broader Impacts / significance sections concretely and specific to
   this proposal (training, outreach, dataset/tool release, diversity
   plans as applicable) rather than generic boilerplate a reviewer has seen
   verbatim before — reviewers weigh specificity.
5. For budget justification, use only figures and effort levels supplied by
   the user or an institutional grants office — never estimate a salary,
   overhead rate, or equipment cost; these are institution- and
   funder-specific and an invented number is a compliance problem, not just
   an inaccuracy.
6. If reviews or a panel summary come back (renewal or resubmission), treat
   the response similarly to `submission-and-peer-review.md`'s referee-
   response guidance: quote or paraphrase each concern, respond factually,
   and mark clearly what changed in the resubmission — but follow the
   funder's own resubmission rules (page limits on the introduction/response
   section are common and often stricter than a journal's).

Deliver the proposal section-by-section against the call's actual required
outline, with `[VALUE NEEDED: ...]` markers for any budget, effort, or
institutional figure not supplied. Do not fabricate letters of support,
collaborator commitments, or institutional matching funds.
