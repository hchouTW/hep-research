# Supplementary-material planning

Use to organize appendices, supplemental files, or supporting artifacts around a
paper's argument. Consult `paper-structure.md` for the main narrative and
`reproducibility-auditing.md` for artifact completeness.

Inventory candidate material and map it to the claim or method it supports.
Distinguish an appendix in the manuscript from a separately submitted supplement
and a data/code repository. Check the target venue's current official rules for
file types, length, review visibility, anonymization, and independent references
when these affect placement; do not assume supplements are exempt from limits.

Keep in the main paper the central result, essential assumptions, core method,
uncertainty, and limitations needed to judge the conclusion. Place extended
derivations, secondary checks, detailed configurations, and additional tables in
supplements when the main account remains understandable and assessable. Do not
hide contradictory evidence or consequential caveats to meet a page limit.

For each item, record proposed location, purpose, main-text anchor, dependencies,
available source, and missing material. Order sections so definitions precede
their uses. Label sections, equations, figures, and tables consistently; ensure
main-text references resolve and identify which file contains each item.

For standalone supplements, supply enough context, notation, and references to
read them independently. Prefer machine-readable data and versioned code/configs
for reusable artifacts; descriptive PDF tables alone may be insufficient. Retain
provenance, units, uncertainty conventions, and version links across formats.

Deliver a proposed outline and placement map, with a short list of missing inputs.
Do not invent supplementary results or move essential material silently. If asked
to perform the reorganization, update both sides of every cross-reference and
verify the rendered main paper and supplement using the available format tools.
