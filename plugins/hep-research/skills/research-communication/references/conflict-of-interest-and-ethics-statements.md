# Conflict-of-interest and ethics statements

Use when drafting a competing-interests/COI disclosure, funding statement,
ethics/IRB-approval statement, animal-research statement, or data-privacy/
consent statement for submission. This is about disclosure accuracy, not who
gets credit for the work — use the authorship-and-contributions section of
`submission-and-peer-review.md` for author order and contribution statements.

1. Establish what the target venue requires. Journals vary in what they
   demand: a standalone Conflicts of Interest / Competing Interests section,
   an ICMJE disclosure form, a funding-statement paragraph, an ethics
   statement citing an IRB/ethics-board approval number, or a data-
   availability-adjacent consent statement for human-subjects data. Check the
   venue's author guidelines rather than assuming one field's convention
   (e.g. HEP collaboration papers rarely carry individual COI statements;
   biomedical and human-subjects work almost always does).
2. Gather disclosures directly from the user or an existing internal
   disclosure record — never infer a financial relationship, funding source,
   or board approval from context, an affiliation, or what seems plausible
   for the field. If information is missing, mark it explicitly
   (`[COI STATUS NEEDED: author 3]`) rather than defaulting to "no conflicts."
3. For funding statements, list the funding body, grant/award number, and
   which author(s) it supports, matching exactly what the user supplies —
   do not merge, round, or drop a grant number for brevity.
4. For ethics/IRB and animal-research statements, confirm the approving
   body's name, protocol/approval number, and whether consent was obtained
   (informed consent, parental consent, waiver) — these are typically
   required verbatim by the venue and cannot be paraphrased without losing
   their function as a compliance record.
5. Check consistency: a COI/funding/ethics statement should not contradict
   the acknowledgments section, author affiliations, or a data/code
   availability statement (e.g. a stated ethics approval should cover the
   dataset actually described in Methods).

Deliver each statement in the venue's required form and location (often a
separate section or a submission-system field, not body text), with any
unresolved disclosure marked rather than assumed absent. Do not represent a
disclosure as complete when an author's response is still outstanding.
