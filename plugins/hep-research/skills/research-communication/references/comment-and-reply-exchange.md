# Comment and Reply exchange

Use when drafting a formal post-publication "Comment" (challenging a
published paper's method, analysis, or conclusion) or a "Reply" to one
(the original authors' response) — the PRL/PRD/JHEP-family article types
built for this, not a referee report or a preprint erratum. Use
`submission-and-peer-review.md` for a pre-publication referee response, and
`erratum-and-corrigendum-drafting.md` when the original authors themselves
found the error rather than a third party disputing the result.

1. Confirm the venue accepts this article type and check its current rules:
   most physics journals cap Comments and Replies to a short length
   (commonly ~1000–1500 words, often shorter than a normal letter), require
   the Comment to focus on a single specific point rather than a general
   critique, and route the Comment to the original authors for a Reply
   before either is published, typically together in the same issue.
2. **Writing a Comment:** state the specific claim being disputed with an
   exact locator (equation, figure, table, or quoted sentence) in the
   original paper. Present the counter-argument or counter-analysis
   precisely — a re-derivation, a reanalysis with stated assumptions, or a
   dataset/comparison the original paper did not consider. Distinguish an
   error that invalidates the conclusion from a limitation the original
   authors already acknowledged; the latter is not Comment-worthy. Do not
   broaden into a general critique of the paper's contribution — venues
   reject Comments that read as competing work rather than a specific
   correction.
3. **Writing a Reply:** address the Comment's specific technical point
   directly — concede it, refute it with reasoning or additional analysis,
   or clarify a misunderstanding of the original method. State plainly
   whether the original result and conclusion stand, are revised, or are
   withdrawn; do not let a Reply read as agreeing with the substance while
   only conceding wording. If the Comment is correct and material, route to
   `erratum-and-corrigendum-drafting.md` for the correction itself — a Reply
   is the venue for the scientific argument, not necessarily the mechanism
   that fixes the archival record.
4. Keep tone factual and specific to the technical point in both directions;
   a Comment/Reply exchange is public and archival — avoid characterizing
   the other party's competence or motives, stick to the physics/statistics
   in dispute.
5. Check cross-referencing: both articles typically cite each other and the
   original paper explicitly, and some venues publish them as a linked pair
   with sequential DOIs — follow the venue's current citation convention
   rather than treating them as independent papers.

Deliver the Comment or Reply as its own short, focused manuscript (not a
patched version of the original paper), with the disputed claim's exact
locator, the technical argument, and a clear statement of what — if
anything — changes about the original result.
